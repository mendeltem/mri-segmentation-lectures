    What am I looking for?

    A microbleed on T2*-weighted MRI is a small, round or oval, dark spot. It is usually 2–10 mm across and you see it on only a few slices.

    Lookalikes – scroll up and down before you click:

    • Vessel: also dark, but it goes on over many slices or runs as a line.
    • Calcification: dark spots in the basal ganglia or the choroid plexus.
    • Air and bone: dark edges at the skull base.

    How to use the viewer

    • Scroll through the slices: mouse wheel, arrow keys ↑ ↓, or the slider.
    • Zoom: the + and − buttons, Ctrl + wheel, or pinch. Move: drag.
    • Click on a microbleed to mark it. Click on the ring again to remove it.

    When is it ONE microbleed? (26-connectivity)

    The expert reference is a set of marked voxels. Voxels that touch by a face, an edge or a corner belong to ONE microbleed. A voxel has 6 + 12 + 8 = 26 such neighbours:

    the voxel F = face (6) E = edge (12) C = corner (8)

    With only the 6 face neighbours, two voxels that touch just by an edge or a corner (small picture) would be counted as two microbleeds. With 26 neighbours they are one.

    Size

    Diameter = diameter of a sphere with the same volume as the microbleed: d = (6·V/π)1/3, with V = number of voxels × voxel volume. Size classes: < 2 mm, 2–5 mm, > 5 mm.

    Data: Where is VALDO? challenge, MICCAI 2021 – Sudre et al., Medical Image Analysis 2024, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2023.103029; https://valdo.grand-challenge.org. Task 2 (microbleeds), T2*-weighted images and expert reference masks.

    How this exercise works

    Three subjects, one after another. The next one opens when you have scored the one before. About 3 minutes each; the clock never blocks you.

    1 Easy→2 Medium→3 Hard→Summary
    1. Scroll through all slices first: mouse wheel, arrow keys ↑ ↓, or the slider. Zoom with + / − or Ctrl + wheel, drag to move.
    2. Look for microbleeds: small, round or oval, dark spots, seen on only a few slices.
    3. Scroll up and down before you click. Lookalikes: a vessel goes on over many slices or runs as a line; calcification sits in the basal ganglia or the choroid plexus; air and bone make dark edges at the skull base.
    4. Click on a microbleed to mark it with a ring. Click the ring again to remove it.
    5. Press Score this subject: you get your F1 score, the microbleeds you found, the ones you missed (with "Jump to"), and your false alarms.
    6. Press Next subject → (at the top or under your result). After the hard subject comes the summary.
